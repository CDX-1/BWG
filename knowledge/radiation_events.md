[RADIATION-EVENTS §1.1]
Europa's magnetospheric environment produces intense radiation flux, particularly during
close flybys. Energetic particle events can produce correlated signatures across multiple
instruments simultaneously.

[RADIATION-EVENTS §2.1]
A genuine external radiation event will produce a temporally correlated response in both
the spectrometer and radiation counter, typically with the radiation counter leading by 0-200ms.
The temporal offset depends on particle species and direction of incidence.

[RADIATION-EVENTS §2.2]
Galactic cosmic ray (GCR) events produce narrow temporal spikes (< 0.5s) in both sensors.
Magnetospheric particle events produce broader signatures (1-10s) with gradual onset and decay.
The 1.8-second duration of the Asteria-7 transient is consistent with a magnetospheric event.

[RADIATION-EVENTS §3.1]
Radiation counter data must be preserved with full time resolution. Averaging or summarizing
the radiation window will destroy temporal correlation information needed to distinguish a
genuine environmental event from an instrument fault.

[RADIATION-EVENTS §3.3]
A 41% increase in radiation counter readings concurrent with a spectrometer transient strongly
suggests an external particle event. However, this association requires confirmation from the
raw radiation time series to rule out coincident electronics noise.
